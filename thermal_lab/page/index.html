Why did usage decrease in the earlier version?

The earlier chart assumed GPUs gained throughput every year. Fewer GPUs could then do the same work. That was a user-model assumption, not a new data point and not a measured QC efficiency gain.

The default now has zero annual growth rates. The 2030–2040 timeline repeats the same source hardware unless you explicitly choose a what-if. GPU technology stays fixed in the default scenario. The QC-routed line can still fall as new routers cover more task assignments; this is a deployment assumption, not Moore’s law.

Moore’s law describes historical transistor-count growth. Neven’s law concerns proposed growth in quantum advantage over classical simulation. Neither gives an automatic annual decrease in fleet power, cost or GPU count. New-QPU power only falls if a power-reduction assumption is explicitly entered.

Sources, proxies and assumptions

Hardware and electricity evidence anchors this optimistic proposal. No source below establishes a 10% QC routing advantage or a fixed assignment capacity per quantum chip. Published facts, borrowed proxies and chosen assumptions are kept distinct.

What this model can establish

  • Reference GPU rack: 144 chips, 792 kW, $23.8M. Source hardware is fixed at 2035. Applying it across 2030–2040 is a rollout illustration, not an annual hardware forecast. Annual trend rates default to zero.
  • Case populations are A: 4 billion prompters, B: 50 million agent developers and C: 400 million knowledge workers. Activity growth changes work per user. Starting rack demand comes from the repository fleet model with beneficial brief levers selected for each case. Speculative decoding is a separate source assumption and is enabled in the recommended scenarios. Extra scientific tool work is added on top, never subtracted from LLM work.
  • QPU prices, jobs/hour, GPU-hours per scientific job, progress rates, installation costs and thermal resistance are editable hypotheses. Quantum jobs/hour must include preparation, repetitions, error correction, data transfer and readout at matched accuracy.
  • The notes describe about 16 MW for the small-chemistry tool QPU; this is a different role from the proposed QC scheduler. The notes do not specify scheduler price, power or decision latency. The optimistic proposal borrows annealer cost and power from external sources, with links in the evidence register. Dispatch latency and thermal values are filled as explicit scenario assumptions, with calibration still required. Unknown costs are not zero.
  • Five-year cost includes purchases, infrastructure, networking, electricity and maintenance. Idle or retired GPU purchases are never refunded. Quantum equipment is kept powered year-round. Future costs are nominal, without financing, inflation or resale.
  • The screenshot allows orbit, Moon and Mars with $200/kg launch cost. This page follows the repository’s Earth-only scope; those locations are excluded. Six sites share capacity equally; local grid limits are unknown.
  • The official scorer/catalog, detailed prefill/context-memory sizing, site prices, service-time distributions and calibrated thermal measurements are still needed. Passing these modeled checks is not certification of challenge compliance.
