The routing opportunity for case B

The team’s fleet model sizes the GPU fleet at about 831 racks before routing improvements. Running background tasks at their required speed and assigning suitable steps to smaller sub-agent models reduces that to about 361 racks: roughly 470 fewer racks, $11.2B less rack purchase cost and 398 MW less facility power before other overheads.

Our proposed architecture uses a QC to make assignment decisions. The capacity saving above is supported by the fleet model; proving that the QC finds better assignments than classical optimization is a separate benchmark. This page keeps that distinction visible.

How the future projection works

This is a scenario calculator: it applies your assumptions year by year. The routing-policy capacity improvement is held constant rather than inventing a new quantum speedup each year. It is not a forecast trained on data, and the progress scenarios are not guaranteed outcomes.

For optional quantum tool processors, 100 accepted jobs/hour with a 25% annual gain becomes 125 after one year and 156.25 after two. Price and power decline independently. Future GPUs also improve; quantum must compete with that future GPU fleet.

The fair question: can hybrid routing serve the same developers, work, accuracy and deadlines at lower cost or energy? The model must be able to show either result.

What this model can establish

  • 2035 catalog GPU rack: 144 chips, 792 kW, $23.8M. Applying those figures in 2030 is an explicit scenario assumption. Annual GPU efficiency improvements apply fleet-wide as a simplified software/hardware throughput assumption; refresh purchases are not modeled.
  • Case B starts at 50 million developers; changing activity growth changes work per developer. Starting rack demand is calculated from the repository fleet model: background speed and sub-agents enabled; speculative decoding and smaller-model chat routing disabled. Extra scientific tool work is added on top, never subtracted from LLM work.
  • QPU prices, jobs/hour, GPU-hours per scientific job, progress rates, installation costs and thermal resistance are editable hypotheses. Quantum jobs/hour must include preparation, repetitions, error correction, data transfer and readout at matched accuracy.
  • Default QPU power is the repository’s illustrative ~16 MW small-chemistry system, not a vendor rating. Purchase price includes its cryostat and internal controls. Facility infrastructure and external cooling are additional; there is no double-counted refrigerator multiplier.
  • Five-year cost includes purchases, infrastructure, networking, electricity and maintenance. Idle or retired GPU purchases are never refunded. Quantum equipment is kept powered year-round. Future costs are nominal, without financing, inflation or resale.
  • The screenshot allows orbit, Moon and Mars with $200/kg launch cost. This page follows the repository’s Earth-only scope; those locations are excluded. Six sites share capacity equally; local grid limits are unknown.
  • The official scorer/catalog, detailed prefill/context-memory sizing, site prices, service-time distributions and calibrated thermal measurements are still needed. Passing these modeled checks is not certification of challenge compliance.
